Just These Buttons

A one-page web app for a grandchild or adult child caring for an elderly grandparent: photograph one machine, name one task, and print a card and number stickers that show only the buttons that task needs.

What scope said, and what we now know

Scope“One tap fixes a wrong button.”
→
PRDTap a lit button to remove it, tap anywhere else to add a step, edit any wording, move steps up or down. Unsure steps arrive flagged Check this one.
Scope“AI locates and orders the needed buttons.”
→
PRDPresses and dial turns, in order, aiming for two or three and never more than five — each step's wording taken from what's printed on the machine.
Scope“A print view: the step card and a sheet of numbered stickers.”
→
PRDOne page that fits A4 and Letter: card on top with the task as its title, sticker strip below with cut lines — still clear from a black-and-white printer.

1 · Open the link

Screens and Layout · First use

The name, a one-line promise, one large Photograph the machine button, and quieter buttons to try a real sample photo (washing machine, microwave, TV remote). One line says the photo goes to an AI service and isn't stored.

  • On a phone, the button offers the camera; on a laptop, a file picker.
  • One tap on the sample reaches a result without taking any photo.

2 · Take the photo

Features and Behavior › Taking the photo

The photo appears on the page, with Retake. Underneath, one question: What should they be able to do?

  • The chosen photo shows within a second.
  • With no task text, Find the buttons is visibly disabled.

3 · Name the task

Features and Behavior › Naming the task

Plain words, the way the family would say it. The same words become the title of the printed card.

  • The task text reappears as the card's title.

4 · The app works

States and Boundaries › Working

The photo stays in view with a calm line saying what it's looking for. Controls wait until it's done. If it fails, the photo and task are kept for another try.

  • Nothing the caregiver entered is lost if the AI can't be reached.

5 · Just these buttons

Features and Behavior › Finding the buttons — the kernel

Everything not needed is dimmed. The needed controls are ringed and numbered in order, with one plain instruction each. A step the AI isn't sure of is marked Check this one and gets a dashed ring.

  • Sample washer + “wash everyday clothes” → two to four steps, each at the right control.
  • Every other control is visibly dimmed.
  • Numbers on the photo match numbers in the list.

6 · The caregiver checks

Features and Behavior › Correcting the result

Tap a lit button to remove it; tap elsewhere to add a step there; rewrite any instruction; move steps up or down. The photo and the list move together.

  • Remove, add and reorder update photo and list together.
  • An edited instruction is exactly what prints.

7 · Print, tape, stick

Features and Behavior › Printing the card and stickers

One page: the card on top, a strip of number stickers below. It goes next to the real machine; the numbers go on the real buttons. Success is the grandparent doing the task from the card, without a phone.

  • Print preview shows exactly one page with card and stickers.
  • No app controls appear on the printout.
  • In black and white, the kept buttons are still clearly marked.

Boundary

Building What the proof of concept must do
  • The one-page flow: photo → task → result.
  • AI finding and ordering the needed controls, with dimming, numbered rings and plain-word steps.
  • Correcting: remove, add, edit, reorder, clear “check this one”.
  • The one-page print layout: step card plus number-sticker strip.
  • The sample washing machine.
  • The states above.
Deferred Implied, not now
  • Cover patches at each button's real size — needs real-world scale; numbered stickers prove the idea.
  • Card in the grandparent's own language — the English card proves the kernel.
  • Reading steps aloud — the card is the product.
  • Saving machines, sharing a PDF or link — would need storage or accounts.
  • Several tasks per card — a second task is a second card.
Non-goals Will not do
  • No live camera or AR guide · no phones or touchscreens · no accounts · no native app.
  • Not a medical or safety tool — it simplifies controls; it doesn't decide what's safe to use.
  • Not for late-stage dementia — the target is early decline, low vision and general confusion with machines.
Open Questions that don't block the spec
  • How reliably can the AI find small buttons, such as on a TV remote? First thing to test in the build; correction tools are the fallback.
  • Which sample photo? Ideally the grandmother's own machine, with permission.
